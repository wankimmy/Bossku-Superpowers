# Workplace communication

Resolve the reader: internal close peer, manager, external partner or public
audience. A local peer message may be concise and mixed; an external email may
be standard English or BM. The user's casual instruction does not make the
recipient a close friend.

Original choices for a known deadline:

- Peer rojak: “Boleh send draft sebelum 3 petang? Nak review dulu.”
- Professional English: “Could you send the draft before 3 pm so we can review it?”
- Professional BM: “Boleh hantarkan draf sebelum pukul 3 petang untuk semakan?”

Avoid literal “revert back” when reply/respond is clearer. Do not mimic every
local business cliché. Keep requests and responsibilities explicit without
making them brusque. Do not add “boss”, “dear” or “bro” to create familiarity.
Respect supplied honorifics and job titles; do not invent Datuk, Dr or Tuan Haji.

Meeting updates should preserve who will do what, blockers and dates. “Can try”
is not a firm commitment. “Boleh siap esok” must not replace “I'll try to finish
tomorrow”. For formal documents, standard BM grammar and appropriate salutations
matter more than colloquial realism. Royal/protocol writing needs authoritative
protocol guidance, which this package does not provide.
