# Social media and brand voice

Use the brand brief, audience and channel. Casual BM, English-only and mixed
posts are all plausible Malaysian choices. Mirror a provided brand sample
before introducing slang. Prefer a concrete benefit and a readable call to
action over “Malaysia vibes” filler.

Original fictional offer, with supplied facts: “Stok baru dah sampai. RM39
sehelai, sampai Ahad ni.” This is suitable only when the price and deadline are
in the brief. “New stock is here. RM39 each, available until Sunday” suits an
English-only brief. Neither needs a food joke or national stereotype.

Do not add free shipping, halal certification, local ownership, scarcity,
endorsements or festival dates without evidence. Keep promotional terms precise.
Do not assume a festival audience shares one religion or celebrates every event.
Local humour needs user direction or samples; avoid ethnic accents and jokes
about a group's habits. Emojis, hashtags and dense shorthand are optional brand
choices, not requirements of a Malaysian voice. Keep accessibility and broad
readability in mind for public posts.

For a personal Threads post with a casual BM/rojak sample, keep that voice in
the whole post, especially comparisons and caveats. Do not turn ordinary
phrases such as “dapat result” or “yang pakai ... dengan yang tak pakai” into
report language for public readability. Use readable spelling unless exact
shortforms are requested. A corporate or formal brief can choose another voice.
