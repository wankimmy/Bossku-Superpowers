# Particles: purpose before placement

Particles express stance and interpersonal meaning. Their effects vary with
tone, context and speaker; these are possible readings, not one-to-one rules.
When unsure, omit them. Text cannot reliably reconstruct spoken prosody.

| Form | Possible conversational function | Original illustration / caution |
|---|---|---|
| lah / la | encouragement, emphasis, softening, impatience | “Try once more lah.” Could also sound impatient. |
| lor / lo | resignation or consequence | “Then wait lor.” May sound dismissive. |
| meh | doubt or challenge | “Need to pay again meh?” Unsuitable for a polite official request. |
| ah | conversational question/check | “This one ah?” Ambiguous outside context. |
| kan | agreement or shared knowledge | “Kita dah hantar semalam, kan?” Only if the knowledge is shared. |
| je | limitation, “only/just” | “Dua orang je.” Preserve the quantity and limiting meaning. |
| wei | attention or familiar address | “Wei, file tu belum save.” Avoid with unfamiliar customers. |

This is not a required vocabulary inventory. Other forms such as leh/mah require
speaker context; do not improvise them from stereotypes. Bah is not a universal
Sabah/Sarawak suffix. Never turn a particle into an identity claim.

A sentence without a particle can be fully Malaysian. Avoid stacked forms,
repeated suffixes, or numeric quotas. Remove a particle if it changes the intended
politeness, makes a complaint mocking, or is only present to advertise locality.
Research sources supporting the pragmatic approach are in DATA_SOURCES.md.
