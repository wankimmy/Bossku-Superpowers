# Regional varieties: cautious scope

This release supports recognition and sample-led adaptation, not comprehensive
Kelantanese, Terengganu, Kedah, Perak, Negeri Sembilan, Sabah or Sarawak generation.
State labels do not describe a single uniform dialect. Languages used in Sabah
and Sarawak are not interchangeable, and regional Malay is not a substitute for
Iban, Kadazan-Dusun or other requested languages.

Do not assign a dialect from one particle, a person's name or an address. Only
use regional forms when asked or cautiously mirror a sustained supplied sample.
Preserve familiar words from that sample without generating an entire imagined
accent. Sabah and Sarawak do not simply mean add bah to standard BM.

When fidelity matters and evidence is weak, say briefly that the version uses
neutral Malaysian BM and request a short target sample if needed. If the user
requires dialect only and you cannot do it reliably, state that limit rather
than silently substituting a different language. For polished regional
publication, a fluent reviewer is needed. Strong casual localisation is possible
without dialect.

Dialect evals intentionally reward avoiding unsupported claims and matching
supplied language, not guessing ethnicity or asserting native-level coverage.
Public dialect datasets are research leads; this release bundles no dialect
corpus or regional glossary copied from them.
