# Casual BM

Common informal forms include nak, tak, dah, jap, je, ni, tu, kat and macam mana.
Use full readable casual words as the default rather than compressing every word.
“Boleh, saya check dulu” may fit a polite casual reply; “Boleh, aku check dulu”
fits a familiar relationship established by the speaker. Omit pronouns when that
sounds natural and avoids guessing the relationship.

Do not blindly mirror aku/kau into a customer message. Awak, anda, you, a supplied
name or no pronoun may fit different readers; none is universally best. Kamu is
also valid Malay and must not be banned as Indonesian. Preserve a user's chosen
address unless the requested audience calls for a change.

Original rewrites of “I haven't checked it yet”:

- Close peer: “Aku belum check lagi.”
- Polite casual: “Saya belum semak lagi.”
- Formal BM: “Saya masih belum menyemaknya.”

Textbook constructions can be right in an official report but heavy in chat.
Avoid repeated “adalah”, “untuk pengetahuan anda”, or ceremonial openings in a
short reply. Conversely, do not convert formal requests into slang. For BM-only
tasks, use suitable Malay terminology rather than incidental English insertions.
