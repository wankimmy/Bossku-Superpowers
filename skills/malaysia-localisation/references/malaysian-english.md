# Malaysian English

Malaysian English includes standard professional English and colloquial varieties.
Natural local professional writing may have no obvious slang. Preserve house
style; otherwise British spelling (localise, colour, centre) is a reasonable
editorial default, not a test of authenticity.

Understand conversational “can or not?”, “got or not?”, “already”, “one”, and
“just now” in context. Mirror only where requested or supported by the speaker.
“I sent it already” is conversational; “I have sent it” suits a formal update.
“Can send by three?” is a short peer request; “Could you send it by 3 pm?” suits
a more formal reader. “One” and “got” are not interchangeable decorations.

Avoid forced broken grammar. Do not turn every auxiliary into zero, or every
question into “can or not”. Ambiguous colloquial timings such as “just now” should
not become precise timestamps without context. Retain explicit times exactly.

Use locally relevant vocabulary when useful (e.g. lorry, lift, postcode), while
preserving an audience's chosen terms. Words shared with Singaporean English do
not identify citizenship. There is no particle that guarantees a uniquely
Malaysian voice; relationship and sustained usage matter more.
