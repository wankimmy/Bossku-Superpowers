# BosskuAI Corporate Training Designer

Use this skill to turn a business problem into a training program that changes on-the-job behaviour, with a plan to measure it. Start from the problem, not from the course catalogue.

## How this differs from nearby skills

- **`onboarding`**: user activation inside a product (signup and first-run flows), not employee training.
- **`bosskuai-recruitment-specialist`**: hiring process, before the person joins.
- **`writing-skills`**: authoring agent skills, not human curricula.
- **`bosskuai-article-writing`**: long-form writing, not instructional design.

## Workflow

1. Diagnose. Ask the business owner for the pain point and the metric it moves. Check whether the root cause is a skill gap or a process, tool, or incentive issue. If it is not a skill gap, say so and stop short of building a course.
2. Define learners and objectives. Write each objective as an observable behaviour with a baseline and a target, for example "share of new hires completing a client proposal alone within 3 months". Avoid vague goals like "improve communication".
3. Gather evidence for gaps: performance data, manager interviews, job task analysis, surveys or focus groups. Prioritise by urgency and importance: must train, should train, can self-learn.
4. Pick a design method. ADDIE for planned programs; SAM (prototype, review, revise) when speed matters. Write objectives at a stated cognitive level (Bloom) and map each to a module and an assessment.
5. Choose the format: online for knowledge, in-person or live for practice, follow-up community for transfer. Flipped, case-based, simulation, and action-learning formats are options. Check the company's existing learning platform and constraints before recommending a tool.
6. Build content with subject-matter experts: convert tacit know-how into steps, cases from real work, practice tasks, and an assessment bank. Package a syllabus, facilitator guide, learner workbook, and slides.
7. Pilot with a small group, collect feedback, revise, then launch.
8. Plan transfer: action plans, manager follow-up, job aids, practice after the session.
9. Evaluate at four levels: reaction, learning, behaviour at set follow-up points, results on the business metric. Choose the deepest level the investment justifies.
10. Report using business metrics, then revise or retire content on a schedule.

## Special tracks

- Onboarding: day-one checklist, buddy or mentor, staged 30/60/90-day goals, required versus elective modules.
- Leadership: tiers by management level, stretch projects, 360 feedback, succession readiness.
- Train-the-trainer: selection criteria, adult-learning basics, delivery practice, observed trial sessions.
- Compliance training: annual plan, attendance records, pass mark, retakes, archive for audit.

## Guardrails

- Compliance, safety, anti-corruption, and privacy content must come from current official policy and law in the relevant jurisdiction; this skill organises the program and records, it is not legal or HR advice. A qualified human (legal, HR, or safety lead) reviews that content before use.
- Use evaluation data to improve training, not to punish individuals. Limit 360 feedback to the person and their direct manager.
- Do not invent ROI or satisfaction numbers; use the organisation's own baselines.
- Do not enrol people, send notices, or change a live learning platform without explicit user approval.

## Output format

```text
Business problem and metric: [text]
Root cause check: [skill gap or not]
Learners and objectives: [behaviour, baseline, target]
Curriculum map: [module, objective, method, assessment]
Format and platform: [blend, constraints]
Pilot plan: [group, feedback]
Evaluation: [levels, timing, owner]
Risks and open questions: [list]
```
